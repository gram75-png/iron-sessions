POWER BLOCK~14 min
01
Two-Hand KB Swing
4 sets x 10 reps
Hip hinge, not a squat - the power comes from snapping the hips, arms just hang on. Hike the bell back aggressively between your legs into your upper inner thighs (like a football snap), then snap the hips forward hard and stand tall - glutes and hamstrings fire, knees straighten. The bell floats to chest height on momentum alone - do not lift it with your arms. At the top, brace into a hard standing plank: glutes clenched, abs braced, ribs down, resist the bell pulling you forward. Weight: single bell, 32-40 kg (70-88 lbs).
Cue: hips explode, float, hinge, repeat. If your knees bend a lot and the bell dips low, you are squatting it - push the hips back, not down. Heels and mid-foot stay planted throughout
Lower back flare-up?Skip entirely until pain-free, then return at 12-16 kg (26-35 lbs) - about a third of working weight - before building back up over 1-2 weeks

Assisted Pull-Up Machine
4 sets x 6-8 reps
Kneel or stand on the assisted pull-up machine platform, hands wide overhand. The counterweight supports part of your bodyweight - full dead hang to chin over the bar every rep. New baseline: 30-35 lbs assist (down from 50 - 6 months at 50 was a habit ceiling, not a strength ceiling). Order sets lightest assist first while fresh, heaviest assist last: e.g. 30 - 30 - 40 - 40.

FLEXION BLOCK~13 min
05
Lying Leg Raise to Candlestick
4 sets x 10-12 reps
Lie flat on your back, hands by your sides or tucked under your lower back. Raise straight legs to vertical, then at the top drive the pelvis off the floor so your legs point straight up at the ceiling (the candlestick). Slow, controlled lower - do not let the lower back arch off the floor on the way down. Target: bodyweight - no added load here, progress by owning full control at 4x12 before adding reps.
The pelvis lift at the very top is the whole point - that final curl of the hips off the floor is where the lower abs actually fire
Lower back flare-up?Skip the pelvis lift and any loaded flexion entirely until pain-free. A gentle Dead Bug (opposite arm/leg reach, low back pressed flat) is a safer core option in the meantime
Too hard?Drop the hip lift and do plain lying leg raises with the back glued to the floor - add the pelvis lift back in once those feel easy, or bend the knees to shorten the lever

SESSION NOTES

  • Pull-up: working assist is now 30-35 lbs (habit ceiling broken after 6 months stuck at 50) - order sets lightest-assist-first while fresh. Retest true ceiling every 2-3 weeks, fresh, not on a fatigued last set
  • KB swing cue - treat the top of every swing as a standing plank
  • Pallof press: pause 2 full seconds at extension - that is where the work happens
  • Lying leg raise to candlestick: the pelvis lift at the top is the key - keep the lower back flat on the descent, no arching
  • RDL: weight is total load - 16-20 kg (35-44 lbs) per bell if using two kettlebells
  • Bird dog is deceptively hard at the end - do not skip it
  • Frequency: 2-3x per week with 48h recovery between sessions

CHEST BLOCK~16 min
01
Incline Plate-Loaded Press
4 sets x 8-10 reps
Set the seat so the handles line up around your upper chest / collarbone. Press to near-full extension without locking the elbows hard, lower under control until you feel a stretch across the pecs. Target: 90-135 lbs per side. Go heavy - this is your main chest builder now. The incline angle biases the upper chest, and independent arms stop your strong side taking over. No balancing demand means you can push close to failure safely.
Handles at upper-chest height - if one arm lags, match the weaker side and let the stronger one catch up over time
Progression:Add a plate per side whenever you hit the top of the rep range with clean form - track it so each side stays balanced

Hands slightly wider than shoulder width. Lower chest to an inch off the floor, press back to full lockout. Body in a rigid plank throughout - this is a core exercise as much as a chest exercise. Target: bodyweight for now - once 3x15 is clean, add a 25-45 lb plate across the upper back.
Squeeze the floor inward as you press - it activates the pecs far more than just pushing up
Want more load?Place a KB or weight plate across your upper back - or elevate feet on a bench for upper chest emphasis

03
KB Single-Arm Press
4 sets x 8 each arm
Bell racked at shoulder, press straight overhead to lockout - controlled up and down, no thrust. Weight: 20-24 kg (44-53 lbs) single bell per arm. The single arm creates a rotational core demand your trunk has to resist. Keep ribs down - do not hyperextend the lower back.
Strict press - no dip, no leg drive. Press and lower under control both directions. Drive your feet into the floor for tension, but knees and hips stay locked; if your heels lift you have turned it into a push press

04
Cable Lateral Raise
3 sets x 12-15 reps
Low pulley, single cable running across the front of your body, handle in the opposite hand. Slight bend in the elbow, raise out to the side to shoulder height leading with the elbow. The cable keeps tension on the side delt at the bottom where a dumbbell gives you nothing - that constant load is the whole point. One arm at a time. Pause 1 sec at the top, slow lower. Weight: 10-20 lbs on the stack - stay strict.
Cross the cable in front so it's already loaded at the bottom. Lead with the elbow, do not flare the wrist up or the front delt takes over

KB Hammer Curl

Controlled all the way down - the eccentric is where the growth happens

07
Rope Tricep Pushdown
3 sets x 12-15 reps
High cable with a rope attachment. Elbows pinned to your ribs, push the rope down and spread the two ends apart at the bottom for a hard tricep squeeze. Control the weight back up - do not let the elbows drift forward. Weight: 40-60 lbs on the stack - pick a load you can spread cleanly at the bottom every rep.
Elbows stay glued to your sides - if they flare or swing you are pulling with the shoulders, not the triceps. The spread at the bottom is where the activation lives
Can also do:Overhead rope extension - same rope, face away from the stack, press up and back to bias the long head of the tricep

08
KB Arnold Press
3 sets x 10 reps
Start with bells at shoulder height, palms facing you. As you press overhead rotate the palms forward. Reverse on the way down. Weight: 12-16 kg (26-35 lbs) PER bell, one in each hand. Hits all three heads of the deltoid in one movement.
Slow the rotation down - the turning motion is where the shoulder benefit comes from

SESSION NOTES

  • Incline plate-loaded press: biases upper chest - load each side evenly and add a plate when you hit the top of the rep range with clean form
  • Single-arm press: if you are swaying to one side significantly, drop the weight - the lean means the load is too heavy
  • Cable lateral raise: go light and strict - the cable holds tension at the bottom, so it will feel harder than a dumbbell at the same weight. Cross the cable in front to load the bottom of the range
  • Curls: both variations hit different parts of the arm - do not skip the hammer curl
  • Rope pushdown: elbows pinned, spread the rope at the bottom - that spread is why you get more tricep activation than the skull crusher
  • Arnold press is a finisher not a heavy lift - control the rotation, do not rush it
  • Pair with the Trunk and Core session - push-pull split means these two complement each other perfectly
  • Frequency: 2x per week with 48h between sessions

  • KB room first - compound movements go before isolation, always
  • Goblet squat: if heels rise, widen stance slightly or place a small plate under heels while you build ankle mobility

  • Leg extension and abductor machine are finishers - lighter weight, feel the contraction
  • Calf raise: full stretch at the bottom every single rep, not just the top half
  • Suggested split: Mon core, Wed legs, Fri push and press

SESSION NOTES

  • Dumbbell version - your no-kettlebell gym build, same shape as the KB push day
  • Incline plate-loaded press: your main chest builder - load each side evenly and add a plate when you hit the top of the rep range
  • Single-arm press: if you are swaying to one side significantly, drop the weight - the lean means the load is too heavy
  • Cable lateral raise: go light and strict - the cable holds tension at the bottom, so it will feel harder than a dumbbell at the same weight. Cross the cable in front to load the bottom of the range
  • Curls: both variations hit different parts of the arm - do not skip the hammer curl
  • Rope pushdown: elbows pinned, spread the rope at the bottom - that spread is why you get more tricep activation than the skull crusher
  • Arnold press is a finisher not a heavy lift - control the rotation, do not rush it
